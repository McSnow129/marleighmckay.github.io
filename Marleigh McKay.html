Who She Is

Artist.
Advocate.
Builder.

Marleigh McKay is a 17-year-old pre-professional dancer, community organizer, visual storyteller, and youth educator based in Washington, DC. She has trained in ballet, pointe, and contemporary dance for over a decade, reaching the pre-professional level while simultaneously building programs that have reached thousands of students across the country.

Her work is unified by a single instinct: to shine light on what has gone unnoticed — in communities, in history, and in the people around her. That instinct has taken her from the studio to the DC City Council chamber, from elementary school classrooms to national conference stages, and from a dance barre to the pages of a children's book read in hundreds of schools nationwide.

Lafayette-Pointer Park

Restoring a Name.
Reclaiming a History.

At twelve years old, Marleigh testified before the DC City Council in support of renaming a park in honor of Captain George Pointer, a formerly enslaved man whose family owned the land for nearly 80 years before being displaced. Selected by the neighborhood civic association based on the strength of her written letter, she spoke with clarity and conviction — not just for herself, but for every student who had ever felt out of place in that space. The name change was approved. At the ribbon cutting ceremony, before hundreds of people, elected officials, and members of the media, she found her little brother in the crowd and spoke directly to him — reminding him that he had a place there.

HBCU Initiatives

Opening Doors
That Weren't There.

After recognizing that her high school had no relationship with Historically Black Colleges and Universities, Marleigh built two programs from scratch. HBCU Day, created as an eighth-grade capstone project, introduced a schoolwide curriculum that has now reached over 2,500 students. The following year, she co-founded her school's first HBCU College Tour, a five-day, seven-campus trip for 27 students that resulted in a 10% increase in HBCU applications. Both programs were designed to continue beyond her involvement. The goal was never visibility. It was access.

The Mindful Practice

Marleigh
Is Mindful.

In first grade, Marleigh's peace teacher, Ms. Linda Ryden, introduced her to the intersection of mindfulness and neuroscience — teaching her not just what to learn, but how to be. That foundation became the framework she has built her life around.

Ms. Ryden was so moved by how deeply the program shaped Marleigh's life that she created a children's book and animated series with Marleigh as its named subject. Marleigh wrote the introduction. That book and the Peace of Mind program now exist in hundreds of schools across the country.

Marleigh regularly visits classrooms to read the book, lead mindfulness exercises, and speak at the annual Peace of Mind national conference — helping students access the same tools that once helped her find her footing.

"She gave me a framework I have built my life around, one I now pass forward to others."
